# BEACON — Sales Script
### The word-for-word script for pitching property managers, built around the Lumina Hollywood demo (1522 Gordon St — 22 stories, 299 units, Morguard-managed; the demo maps their real tower and floor plans).

**Format:** 15-minute in-person or video meeting with a Property Manager / Regional Manager.
**Bring:** laptop or tablet with `index.html` open (HUD tab), your Meta glasses on the table as a prop — not on your face.
**Rule of the meeting:** you are an ex-leasing agent first, a founder second. Sell the pain, not the tech.

---

## 0. The one-liner (say it exactly)

> "BEACON puts your entire vacancy inventory in your leasing agent's field of view. A prospect describes what they want, and the agent sees every matching unit in the building — instantly, ranked, with a route to walk there. It's the software Anduril would build if it cared about apartments instead of battlefields."

---

## 1. Cold open — the vacancy math (90 seconds)

Don't open with the product. Open with their bleeding.

> "Before I show you anything — quick math on your building. What's your average rent on a two-bed here?"

*(Let them answer. Say it's ~$4,000.)*

> "So every vacant two-bed costs you about **$130 a day**. Industry average vacant time is now **34 days between leases** — RealPage's number, not mine. That's **~$4,500 per turn, per unit**, in pure lost rent. And right now **around 40% of LA listings are offering concessions**, so every extra vacant day also pressures you into giving away free weeks.
>
> I was a leasing agent. I know exactly where those days hide: they hide in the tour. Every tour, I burned the first ten minutes doing the same thing — digging through the PMS trying to figure out *which* vacancies actually fit the person in front of me. Multiply that across **8 to 10 tours a day**, and your agents spend a quarter of their selling time being a search engine.
>
> I built the thing I wish I'd had."

---

## 2. Three discovery questions (2 minutes)

Ask, then shut up and listen. Their answers become your demo emphasis.

1. **"When a prospect sits down and says 'two bed, high floor, we have a dog' — walk me through what your agent physically does next."**
   *(They'll describe PMS screens, printed availability sheets, asking a colleague. That's your enemy.)*
2. **"How many tours can your best agent run on a Saturday before quality drops?"**
   *(Industry ceiling is 8–10. Whatever they say, BEACON's pitch is: same agent, more tours, better tours.)*
3. **"How do you track units coming vacant 30–60 days out — and do your agents actually sell those on tours?"**
   *(Almost nobody sells future availability well. BEACON makes upcoming vacancies visible inventory.)*

## 3. The demo (6 minutes)

### Beat 1 — Agent HUD, first query
*Open the demo, HUD tab. Click the chip: **"Two bedroom, top floor."***

> "This is what my agent sees — on a screen today, and in the lens of Meta display glasses as that hardware opens up. Watch: the prospect just said 'two bedroom, top floor.'
>
> One second. The whole building lights up. Best fit first: **Residence 2402**, your 1,440-square-foot 2x2D corner on the top floor, ready now, 93% match — locked on with a targeting reticle *on the building itself*, so the agent knows exactly where it is, which face it's on, and the walk time to get there. Second and third choices right behind it, including one that's mid-turn and ready August 8th — inventory your agents usually forget exists."

*Click **Guide** on the top card.*

> "One tap and it routes the tour: lobby, Elevator B, floor 23, left off the elevator. The agent never breaks eye contact with the prospect. The tour starts *immediately* — while your competitor's agent is still squinting at Yardi."

### Beat 2 — the dog query (personalization)
*Click **"One bed — we have a dog."***

> "Now the human stuff. Dog owner? BEACON knows your amenity deck is on 5 — pool, fitness, dog run — so the top match is **611, one floor up from the dog run**, and because Lumina takes pets with no deposits and no weight limits, BEACON says that right on the card. That's the detail that makes a prospect feel *understood*, and it happened in under a second, from a brand-new agent on day one. Your ten-year veteran's building knowledge, downloaded into every hire. With **leasing staff turnover running 33–40%** and the average leasing consultant staying under a year, that institutional memory is worth real money."

### Beat 3 — Fair Housing Guard (the trust moment)
*Click **"Penthouse with a den, no kids around."***

> "And here's the part your compliance team will love. Prospect says something like 'no kids around' — BEACON **refuses to use it.** See the banner: familial status is protected under the Fair Housing Act, so the request is ignored and matching runs on unit criteria only. Your agents can't accidentally steer, because the system won't let them. I'll take that to your legal counsel personally."

### Beat 4 — Ops Console (the manager's screen)
*Switch to the Ops tab.*

> "This is your view. Live stacking plan of all 299 homes — occupied, vacant-ready, in turn, on notice. Your **monthly exposure in dollars**, not unit counts: this building is carrying about **$41,700 a month** in empty rent. Click any unit — here's 2402: six days on market, **$172 a day of carry cost**, one button sends it straight to the agent's HUD for the next tour. Your 60-day notice pipeline becomes sellable inventory instead of a spreadsheet."

### Beat 5 — the glasses (the closer)
*Pick the glasses up off the table now. If you have a Ray-Ban Display with developer mode set up, hand them over. Otherwise show BEACON GLASS (`glass.html`) full-screen.*

> "Now the part nobody else in this industry has. In May, Meta opened the Ray-Ban Display to developers — apps can now render *inside the lens*. This is **BEACON GLASS**, my in-lens app. It's not a mockup; it runs on these glasses today in developer mode. The agent flicks a finger on the Neural Band — that wristband — and cycles requests, units, and turn-by-turn routing to the residence, floating in their vision while they keep full eye contact with your prospect.
>
> Full transparency on where Meta is: today this installs by joining my developer tester list — up to a hundred testers, perfect for a pilot team. Meta's public app distribution opens later this year, and voice is first-party-only for now, so the agent uses gestures, not speech. That's the honest state of the platform. But understand what it means: when public distribution flips on, every operator in LA will discover this exists. The only question is whether your building is the case study or the follower."

## 4. Objection handling

**"We already have an interactive map on our website (SightMap)."**
> "Keep it — that's a *prospect-facing marketing* tool, and Engrain is great at it. BEACON is *agent-facing selling*: natural-language matching, ranked fits, upcoming vacancies, routing, fair-housing guardrails, in the agent's eyes mid-conversation. Different job, different moment. SightMap works before the tour; BEACON works during it."

**"Our PMS won't allow another integration."**
> "BEACON reads availability — it never writes to your system of record. For the pilot we don't even need an integration: a nightly availability export gets us live in a week. If you're on Entrata, their open API makes it same-week. Yardi and RealPage take longer through their partner programs, which is exactly why we start with the export."

**"Glasses are a gimmick."**
> "Maybe! Which is why nothing I showed you requires them. The web HUD alone cuts unit-matching from minutes to seconds on hardware you already own. But 'gimmick' is worth pressure-testing: Meta sold **over 7 million smart glasses last year**, the Display model is **$799 with the Neural Band included**, and it's supply-constrained with waitlists — this is iPhone-2008 territory. When your prospects start wearing them, your leasing office will already speak the language."

**"Fair housing risk — an algorithm choosing units scares me."**
> "That fear is exactly why the guard exists. BEACON matches on unit attributes only — floor, plan, price, dates, amenity distance. Protected-class information is detected and *visibly discarded*, with a log. Compare that to the status quo: a tired agent improvising at 4pm on their ninth tour. Which one survives an audit better?"

**"What does it cost?"**
> "Less than one day of vacancy per month. For a building this size, $199 a month plus a dollar a unit — about $330 for this tower. If BEACON saves you **three vacant days a year on one unit**, it's paid for itself. And the pilot is free, so the risk is mine."

## 5. The close — the pilot ask (60 seconds)

> "Here's what I'd like to do. **60-day free pilot, this building.** I load your availability, train your team — it's a 20-minute training, it's a search bar — and we measure three numbers together:
>
> 1. **Time from 'here's what I want' to first unit shown** — today that's minutes; I'm betting under 15 seconds.
> 2. **Tours per agent per day.**
> 3. **Days-vacant on units leased during the pilot** vs your trailing average.
>
> If the numbers move, we sign at $199 + $1/unit monthly and you're the reference building — every LA operator who sees this after you will be told 'Lumina ran it first.' If they don't move, I delete the data, shake your hand, and you got a free experiment. Who besides you needs to be in the room to say yes to that?"

*(That last question surfaces the real decision-maker — regional manager, owner, or asset manager — before you leave.)*

### Delivery notes
- **Never oversell the glasses.** The honesty in Beat 5 ("the moment Meta opens in-lens rendering") builds more trust than a fake claim. You're selling a working web product with a hardware roadmap, not vaporware.
- **Let them drive.** After the scripted chips, hand them the keyboard: "type what your last prospect asked for." The moment a PM types their own query and sees their building respond is the moment they buy.
- **The prop matters.** Glasses stay on the table until Beat 5. Picking them up is the physical punctuation of the roadmap.
